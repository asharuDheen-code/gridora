How to Size Storage for a Commercial Site

Sizing starts with a year of interval data, not a capacity number. Working from the load profile backwards to power, energy and the tariff the system has to beat.

Start with interval data

The first thing to ask for is not a capacity requirement but the meter data: fifteen- or thirty-minute interval readings, ideally for a full year. A month will show the shape. A year shows the seasonality, and seasonality is where sizing usually goes wrong.

If the site has on-site generation, generation data matters as much as consumption. Net load is what the system actually sees.

What to send us

A year of interval data as a CSV, your current tariff, and a single-line diagram if one exists. That is enough for a first-pass sizing.

Separate power from energy

These are two independent decisions and conflating them is the most common sizing mistake.

Power is how much the system has to deliver at once. For peak shaving it is the gap between your target threshold and the highest interval you need to cover. Power sizes the conversion stage.

Energy is power multiplied by how long the peak lasts, plus margin for efficiency and for the depth of discharge you are prepared to use. Energy sizes the racks.

A site with a sharp thirty-minute peak needs high power and little energy. A site that runs above the threshold for three hours needs the opposite. Same annual consumption, very different systems.

Let the tariff set the target

The threshold you shave to is an economic choice, not a technical one. Lowering it further saves more on demand charges but requires disproportionately more energy, because the intervals get wider as you go down.

Plot avoided demand charge against system size and the curve flattens somewhere. That knee is usually the right answer, and it is rarely the largest system that fits.

Sanity checks before quoting

  • Does the site have physical space, including access and separation distances?
  • Can the existing switchboard accept the connection, or does that need work too?
  • What is the worst-case ambient temperature, and does the cooling design cover it?
  • Is the metering point the same one the tariff is calculated from?
  • Will the load profile still look like this in three years, given known plans?

Three sizing mistakes worth avoiding

Sizing on annual consumption. Total kilowatt-hours tell you almost nothing about the shape, and shape is what storage responds to.

Ignoring the recharge window. A system that discharges into the evening peak has to recharge before the next one. If the cheap window is short, that constrains power as much as the discharge does.

Designing for the best month. Sizing on a summer profile and discovering the winter one is different is an expensive way to learn about seasonality.
