Scope

A 20 MWh, 10 MW grid-forming system serving a campus with its own distribution network, existing rooftop and ground-mount solar, and a standby generator. The objective was extended islanded operation and a substantial reduction in generator run-hours.

About this entry

An illustrative newsroom entry rather than an account of a real project. It describes the commissioning sequence such a system would follow.

The sequence

Commissioning ran over six weeks, in a deliberate order:

  1. Static checks. Insulation, polarity, torque verification and protection settings, all before anything was energised.
  2. Auxiliary energisation. Cooling, controls and communications brought up and proven independently of the power path.
  3. First DC energisation. Rack by rack, with insulation monitoring live throughout.
  4. Grid-following operation. Charge and discharge against the grid at progressively higher power, checking thermal behaviour at each step.
  5. Grid-forming transfer. Planned island of a limited section of the campus, then the full campus.
  6. Witnessed test. Unplanned-style island and closed-transition return, observed and documented with the client present.

What the tests surfaced

The islanding transfer itself was uneventful. Two items did need attention. The solar inverters needed their reconnection timing adjusted so they did not all return simultaneously after an island event and overshoot the storage's absorption capability. And the generator start threshold, originally set on a timer, was moved to a state-of-charge floor combined with a forecast check — which is what the site actually wanted.

Handover

Documentation issued at handover covered as-built drawings, protection settings as commissioned, the witnessed test record, and the operating envelope written in plain language for the campus facilities team rather than only as controller parameters.
